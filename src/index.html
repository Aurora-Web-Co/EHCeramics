Present

I'm a ceramicist based in Scotland. I make work by hand, and find most of my inspiration somewhere between the studio and the coast.

I studied ceramics at Grays School of Art, where I developed a practice rooted in material honesty and careful making. I was awarded for my work there, and it gave me the foundation to take the craft seriously.

I now work as Lead Ceramicist running the studio and teaching people at all stages of their practice. I find the teaching side genuinely rewarding - watching someone develop a feel for clay is one of those things that doesn't get old. Alongside that, I continue to develop my own collections, while experimenting within my own practice

This site is home to that work. If something resonates, I'd love to hear from you.

Work & Collections

I work in small runs - each piece thrown by hand and finished at its own pace. Nothing is exactly repeated. What you'll find here is my current work.

The Tea Set

This is a kyusu and four yunomi - a Japanese side-handled teapot and its cups, made to be used together. The form is rooted in Japanese tea culture, but the clay and the making are entirely my own.
The body is dark stoneware, left largely unglazed so the texture does the work. I carved the surface of each cup by hand - the same rhythm of marks across all four, though no two came out quite the same. That felt right for something meant to be shared. The glaze sits only at the rim, pooling slightly where it meets the raw clay - that boundary between finished and unfinished is something I find myself returning to a lot.
The kyusu shape suits the way I think about tea - unhurried, considered, made for pouring slowly. There's something about a side handle that asks you to slow down and hold it properly.
This is a functional set, made to be used. The pieces will develop their own character over time, which is part of the point.

Urn Set

A collection of hand-thrown ceramic vessels exploring form, texture, and restraint. These large-scale pieces combine smooth matte finishes with finely carved detailing around the rim, creating a balance between precision and organic simplicity.
Photographed within a natural landscape, the work reflects an ongoing dialogue between contemporary craft and the quiet rhythms of the environment.
